# What is OVA

**OVA (Original Video Animation) is a term for anime that is produced for direct release to the home video market, rather than for initial broadcast on television or in theaters.**

An OVA is a self-contained anime title, typically ranging from a single episode to a multi-part series, created specifically for distribution on physical media like VHS, Laserdisc, DVD, or Blu-ray. The format originated in Japan in the early 1980s, offering creators a distinct alternative to the constraints of TV broadcasting. Without the need to adhere to strict TV time slots, censorship standards, or weekly production schedules, OVAs often feature higher production values, more experimental storytelling, and content aimed at niche audiences, including more mature themes, complex narratives, or detailed animation that would be impractical for a weekly series.

The direct-to-video model allows for flexibility in episode length and release schedule. An OVA series might release episodes months apart. This format has been used to adapt manga story arcs that don't fit into a TV series, create side stories set in existing anime universes, or produce original works deemed too risky for mass broadcast. Notable examples include the groundbreaking *Dallos* (1983), considered the first OVA, the cyberpunk classic *Bubblegum Crisis*, and the influential *Tenchi Muyo!* series.

For anime fans and within a directory like bestanime.info, OVAs are significant because they often represent premium or essential content. They are frequently where creators have the most artistic freedom, leading to cult classics and deeply beloved entries in a franchise. Use cases include: **Filling in Gaps** (e.g., the *Attack on Titan: No Regrets* OVA, which provides backstory), **Alternative Adaptations** (e.g., the more manga-faithful *Hellsing Ultimate*), and **Standalone Experiments** (e.g., the avant-garde *FLCL*, which was originally produced as a six-episode OVA series). Understanding a title's format as an OVA sets expectations for its pacing, scope, and potential audience.

**Related Terms:** **ONA (Original Net Animation)** refers to anime released directly online, the modern evolution of the OVA model. **Special** is a term sometimes used interchangeably but often denotes a one-off episode aired on TV or released with a film, rather than a video-first production.
